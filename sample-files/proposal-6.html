EXECUTIVE
SUMMARY

BEEAH Group stands at a defining inflection point. Two chapters of remarkable growth — establishing waste management dominance, then diversifying into real estate and investments — have created a conglomerate of genuine regional consequence. Chapter 3 demands reconsolidation: aligning three autonomous business units around the dual ambition of becoming a global waste-to-energy leader and anchoring Sharjah’s socioeconomic transformation.

The challenge is structural and cultural. Business unit incentives reward independence over integration. A pattern of strategic whiplash — driven by the Group CEO’s visionary ideation outpacing the organisation’s absorption capacity — has bred a “wait and see” culture among division leaders. Executive buy-in is uneven. Capability gaps persist.

EMIR has been asked to provide advisory support across four integrated workstreams. First, strategic implementation support to realign BU incentives, KPIs, and leadership capabilities with Chapter 3 objectives. Second, the design of an innovation unit that serves as a structural buffer — absorbing the CEO’s ideas, filtering them against strategic fit, and cascading validated prototypes at a pace the organisation can sustain. Third, AI enablement of the Executive Office to strengthen group coordination. Fourth, the design and facilitation of the March leadership retreat as the catalytic moment for executive enrollment.

We propose not a consulting engagement but a strategic partnership — bringing EMIR’s unique network of global advisors and experts to bear on the structural, cultural, and leadership dimensions of BEEAH’s most consequential transition. The engagement spans 12 weeks, culminating in the March retreat and the operational launch of the innovation unit.

CONTEXT &
OBJECTIVES

BEEAH Group’s trajectory from a Sharjah-based waste management authority to a multi-vertical conglomerate spanning environment, real estate, and investments is a remarkable story of ambition matched by execution. The group processes over 3 million tonnes of waste annually, operates the region’s most advanced waste-to-energy facility, and has expanded into Saudi Arabia and Egypt — all under visionary leadership that consistently sees further than the market.

But the conditions that enabled Chapters 1 and 2 are precisely the conditions that now create friction in Chapter 3. Autonomous business units, empowered by their own P&Ls and incentive structures, have built operational cultures optimised for independence rather than integration. The emergence of Tadweer — ADQ’s direct competitive response to BEEAH’s regional dominance — has raised the strategic stakes. BEEAH’s 10x growth ambition over the next decade requires a group that moves as one, not three.

The March leadership retreat represents the first formal opportunity to enroll the full executive team in this new chapter. The window to shape it — and to put the structural foundations in place — is now.

SCOPE OF
WORK

Workstream 1: Strategic Implementation Support — Business Unit Alignment

Objective: Align the incentive structures, KPIs, cultural attitudes, and leadership capabilities of BEEAH’s three business units with the Chapter 3 group strategy.

1.1 Diagnostic & Assessment (Weeks 1–4)

  • Conduct structured interviews with each business unit CEO and their direct reports to assess current understanding of, and commitment to, the Chapter 3 strategy
  • Map existing incentive structures (compensation, bonus triggers, KPIs, performance review criteria) across all three divisions to identify specific points of misalignment with group objectives
  • Assess inter-BU coordination mechanisms — where they exist, where they are absent, and where current incentives actively discourage collaboration
  • Evaluate leadership capability gaps within each BU executive team against the competencies required by Chapter 3 (global expansion readiness, cross-BU collaboration, innovation absorption capacity)
  • Deliver a confidential diagnostic report to the Group CEO and Executive Office

1.2 Incentive Realignment Framework (Weeks 4–8)

  • Design a recommended incentive realignment framework that introduces group-level KPIs alongside BU-specific targets — calibrated to drive inter-BU coordination without undermining divisional accountability
  • Propose shared-success mechanisms (e.g., cross-BU project bonuses, group EBITDA multipliers, shared services efficiency dividends) that make reconsolidation tangibly rewarding for BU leaders
  • Recommend specific changes to executive compensation structures, performance review processes, and reporting cadences
  • Stress-test the framework against each BU CEO’s likely objections and resistance patterns

1.3 Executive Enrollment Plan (Weeks 4–8, feeding into Workstream 4)

  • Develop a tailored enrollment strategy for each of the three BU CEOs — identifying what Chapter 3 means specifically for their division, their career, and their legacy
  • Design a structured engagement sequence (pre-retreat conversations, retreat sessions, post-retreat commitments) that builds genuine buy-in rather than performative compliance
  • Address the “wait and see” culture directly: propose commitment mechanisms and accountability structures that signal Chapter 3’s permanence

1.4 Capability Development Recommendations (Weeks 6–10)

  • Based on the diagnostic, recommend targeted interventions — executive coaching, strategic hires, team restructuring, or development programmes — to close identified capability gaps
  • Identify specific roles or competencies that must be recruited externally to support the global expansion ambition (particularly in waste-to-energy, international market entry, and digital transformation)

Workstream 2: Innovation Unit Design — The Strategic Buffer

Objective: Design and stand up an innovation/prototyping unit that serves as a structured intermediary between the Group CEO’s visionary ideation and the business units’ operational execution — absorbing, filtering, and translating ideas into actionable prototypes at a pace the organisation can sustain.

2.1 Operating Model Design (Weeks 2–6)

  • Define the innovation unit’s mandate, positioning, and relationship to the Group CEO, Executive Office, and business units — clarifying what it is (a rapid prototyping and idea-filtering function) and what it is not (a fourth business unit or a shadow executive office)
  • Design the unit’s operating model: team composition, skills profile, reporting lines, physical/virtual location, and interaction protocols with each BU
  • Establish the “idea intake” process: how the Group CEO’s concepts enter the unit, how they are assessed against strategic fit and feasibility, and how they are prioritised
  • Define the “prototype-to-cascade” process: how validated prototypes are handed off to business units for scaling, including knowledge transfer, resource allocation, and accountability handover mechanisms

2.2 Governance & Budget Framework (Weeks 4–8)

  • Design the governance structure: decision rights, escalation paths, kill criteria for prototypes, and regular review cadences with the Group CEO and BU CEOs
  • Propose an annual budget framework and funding mechanism (ring-fenced group allocation vs. BU co-funding vs. project-specific budgets)
  • Define success metrics for the unit itself — speed of idea assessment, prototype completion rates, BU adoption rates, and strategic impact measures

2.3 Launch Roadmap (Weeks 6–10)

  • Develop a phased launch plan: founding team recruitment, initial project selection (recommend the AI enablement of the Executive Office as the inaugural project — see Workstream 3), and first 90-day milestones
  • Identify quick wins that demonstrate the unit’s value to both the Group CEO and the BU CEOs within the first quarter of operation
  • Design communication and branding for the unit that positions it as an enabler, not a competitor, to the business units

Workstream 3: AI Enablement of the Executive Office

Objective: Diagnose and implement AI-powered tools and workflows that materially improve the Executive Office’s capacity to coordinate across BEEAH Group — and serve as the innovation unit’s proof-of-concept project.

3.1 AI Enablement Diagnostic (Weeks 2–4)

  • Map the Executive Office’s current workflows, information flows, decision-support processes, and coordination mechanisms with the three BUs
  • Identify the highest-impact opportunities for AI augmentation — likely including: automated strategic intelligence briefings, cross-BU performance dashboards, meeting preparation and follow-up automation, and group-wide initiative tracking
  • Assess current technology infrastructure and data availability constraints

3.2 AI Roadmap & Tool Selection (Weeks 4–6)

  • Develop a prioritised AI enablement roadmap for the Executive Office, sequenced by impact and implementation complexity
  • Recommend specific tools and platforms (commercial, custom, or hybrid) for each identified use case
  • Define data requirements, integration needs, and security/confidentiality parameters

3.3 Pilot Implementation (Weeks 6–12)

  • Implement the first two to three AI tools/workflows as a hands-on pilot
  • Train Firas, Nurhan, and the Executive Office team on adoption and daily use
  • Measure impact against pre-defined metrics (time saved, decision quality improvement, coordination effectiveness)
  • Document the implementation process as a replicable case study for the innovation unit’s future projects

Workstream 4: March Leadership Retreat — Design & Facilitation

Objective: Design and facilitate the March leadership retreat as a catalytic moment that enrolls BEEAH’s senior executive team in Chapter 3 — moving from awareness to ownership.

4.1 Retreat Design (Weeks 1–{{TBD: weeks before retreat}})

  • Collaborate with the Executive Office to define retreat objectives, participant list, and desired outcomes
  • Design a retreat agenda that balances strategic alignment sessions, BU-specific breakouts, innovation unit introduction, and leadership bonding — structured to surface and address resistance constructively
  • Develop pre-retreat engagement materials (strategic briefing packs, diagnostic findings summaries, provocation papers) that ensure participants arrive prepared and oriented
  • Incorporate insights from Workstream 1 diagnostic findings to target specific alignment gaps and enrollment challenges

4.2 Facilitation Delivery (March {{TBD: exact date}})

  • Facilitate the full retreat programme on-site in {{TBD: retreat location}}
  • Lead strategic alignment discussions, innovation unit unveiling, and commitment-building exercises
  • Capture decisions, commitments, and open items in real-time

4.3 Post-Retreat Momentum (Week following retreat)

  • Deliver a retreat summary and commitment tracker to the Group CEO and Executive Office within 48 hours
  • Recommend a 90-day post-retreat accountability cadence to sustain momentum and convert retreat commitments into operational reality

  2. Executive enrollment: All three BU CEOs articulate and commit to a specific Chapter 3 contribution plan by end of the March retreat
  3. Innovation unit operational: Founding team in place and first prototype project (AI enablement) underway within 60 days of operating model approval
  4. Executive Office AI adoption: Minimum three AI-powered workflows in daily use by Firas and Nurhan’s team within 12 weeks
  5. Strategic whiplash reduction: Measurable decrease in ad-hoc strategic pivots reaching BU operational level, with innovation unit absorbing ≥ 70% of new CEO-originated concepts within its first six months

METHODOLOGY

Workstream 1: - Confidential diagnostic report: BU incentive structures, capability gaps, and buy-in assessment - Incentive realignment framework with implementation recommendations - Tailored executive enrollment plan for each BU CEO - Capability gap remediation recommendations (coaching, hiring, restructuring)

Workstream 2: - Innovation unit operating model (mandate, structure, team, processes) - Governance and budget framework - Prototype-to-cascade process design - Phased launch roadmap with 90-day milestones

Workstream 3: - AI enablement diagnostic and prioritised roadmap - Tool selection and architecture recommendations - Pilot implementation of two to three AI workflows - Executive Office team training and adoption support

Workstream 4: - Leadership retreat agenda and facilitation plan - Pre-retreat strategic briefing materials - On-site facilitation delivery - Post-retreat summary, commitment tracker, and 90-day accountability framework

Our approach is built on three principles that distinguish EMIR from traditional management consultancies:

Direct access, not delegated research. Every diagnostic conversation, facilitation session, and design workshop will be led by senior EMIR partners — not junior analysts assembling frameworks from secondary sources. We speak directly with the principals who hold the power and the context.

Network-powered insight. We will draw on our unique network of global advisors and experts — including individuals with direct experience in global waste management leadership, GCC sovereign conglomerate governance, corporate innovation unit design, and AI-enabled executive operations. These are not theoretical inputs; they are practitioners who have built and led the structures we are proposing.

Embedded collaboration, not external prescription. We will work alongside Firas, Nurhan, and the Executive Office as embedded partners — not as external consultants delivering reports from a distance. Design sessions will be co-creative. Recommendations will be stress-tested in real-time with the people responsible for implementing them.

Specific methods include: - Structured one-on-one interviews with all BU CEOs and select direct reports - Executive Office workflow shadowing and AI readiness assessment - Facilitated design workshops for the innovation unit operating model - Benchmarking against comparable innovation structures in GCC sovereign entities and global environmental services firms - On-site retreat facilitation with pre- and post-retreat engagement sequences
